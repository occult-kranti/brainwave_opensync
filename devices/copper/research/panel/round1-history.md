## Two concrete errors that should not survive integration

**Gateway’s dimensional step.** Section 19 (GovInfo PDF page 14, printed page 12) compares an oscillator speed in cm/s with a distance in cm. That is dimensionally invalid. For the modern countermodel `q=A cos(ωt)`, `q̇=−Aω sin(ωt)` reaches zero at an ordinary turning point while displacement is maximal. Nothing in this solution exits its time coordinate. A length scale cannot become a speed threshold without an independently specified time scale and physical law. This refutes that derivation; it does not measure or rule on all subjective experiences. [H1]

**The Gateway companion’s rebuttal is also overbroad.** `archive/FREQUENCY-RESEARCH.md` uses an “audible photons” argument and a universal tissue `Q≈1`. Sound is mechanical; `hf` would be a quantum energy, not the total energy delivered by a classical acoustic field. Total energy and response depend on amplitude, duration, coupling and dissipation. A small quantum does not itself forbid a macroscopic mechanical effect. Nor is one unspecified Q a material-independent bound across modes and frequencies. Replace this argument with a requirement for measured dose, transfer, selectivity and endpoint evidence; do not generalize therapeutic-ultrasound results to listening presets. [H7]

The preset’s 200/207.83 Hz difference is exact arithmetic under its digital specification. The label “7.83 Hz is exact geophysics” is false precision: measured Schumann peaks and cavity eigenfrequencies are not one universal exact constant. An acoustic binaural difference also does not constitute a calibrated 7.83 Hz Earth-like electromagnetic exposure. The existing A/D badge conflates arithmetic, geophysics and a causal claim. [H7–H9]

## A useful hypothesis with an actual falsifier

**H-observation:** for an unchanged linear specimen/fixture state, changing excitation/readout geometry can change modal residues and visible peak maxima while the same complex poles remain common across channels.

Use the existing linear model as the finite synthetic baseline:

`H_g(ω)=c_gᵀ[K−ω²M+iωC]⁻¹b_g`.

Geometry index `g` changes the input/output vectors. It does not change M, C or K in this restricted hypothesis. Fit several calibrated complex FRFs jointly, with common poles and channel-specific residues. Freeze a mode band, fit model/order, smallest meaningful shift, calibration/uncertainty method and held-out sensor geometry before inspecting the location contrast. Acquire returns, movement shams, empty fixture, reference channel and matched support/orientation/temperature controls. Fit both shared-pole and geometry-specific-pole models and check held-out predictions, not only in-sample residual improvement.

**Falsifier:** repeated channel-consistent pole changes exceed the prespecified combined uncertainty, the shared-pole model fails held-out complex responses, and these failures persist when geometry alone is varied with the presumed state held fixed. This rejects H-observation’s fixed-dynamics premise. It does not establish an absolute-location or consciousness cause: contact, heating, nonlinear response and model incompleteness are separate candidates. Conversely, if the apparent shift follows residues while poles remain common, the strongest-peak interpretation fails.

For a proposed genuinely position-dependent term, parameterize it explicitly, e.g. `K=K₀+ε h(x_lab)K₁`, with ε dimensionless, h dimensionless and K₁ in N/m. This is a **model deformation**, not an observed field. Ordinary support stiffness, temperature, drive and sensor parameters remain nuisance quantities. Before fitting ε, inspect the sensitivity/Jacobian rank and profile likelihood: if ε is degenerate with a support or thermal parameter, the current data cannot identify it. An arbitrarily flexible h cannot be learned as a new law from two positions.

1. Theuss et al., corrected PRL 132, 066003 (2024), explicitly tie RUS frequencies to density, elasticity and boundaries; their uncertainty section names transducer loading, alignment and density errors. Measured geometry and a credible forward model are useful additions. Their material-specific fitted tensor is not a universal frequency address. Read: introduction and methods through measurement uncertainty, with reported cross-solver comparison. [H10]
2. Kober, Zeman and Scalerandi, online 17 August 2026 (January 2027 issue), report fixed-phase NRUS tracking and sensitivity to protocol/history on sandstone. This supports comparing complex phase and measurement order; it does not certify copper performance. Only the primary abstract and date metadata were inspected. [H11]
3. Sátori et al. (2024) distinguish measured Schumann peak frequencies from lossy-cavity eigenfrequencies and analyze source/observer dependence. That is a particularly useful cross-domain connection: even a global electromagnetic resonance is observed through a source, medium and receiver. It supports the measurement lesson, not a common body/Earth mechanism. [H8–H9]
4. JCGM guidance supplies correlated-input uncertainty and the official register now lists the 2026 nonlinearity amendment. The paired A/B covariance model is useful within its stated local-linear assumptions. It does not price an omitted physical effect into existence. No new software or external solver was executed for this seat. [H12]

This is a relevance-bounded check, not an exhaustive latest-literature survey. No new AI benchmark is needed to establish these measurement principles.
