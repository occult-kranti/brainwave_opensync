## Exact common-driver objection

The selected observational packet X=z, Y=2z supports both descriptions Y=2X and Y=2z. In the centered five-row design [X,z], the columns are equal: rank 1, nullity 1. The coefficient perturbation (1,−1) changes the proposed allocation between direct coupling and common driver without changing any prediction. Having five observations and two named columns does not make them independent evidence.

This is precisely why a common calendar signal can connect Schumann readings, activity schedules and symbolic classifications in a graph without identifying a physical transmission path. Adjustment cannot recover a unique direct coefficient from perfectly collinear variables. Renaming z “planetary influence” supplies no new observation and does not change the kernel.

The hypothetical perturbation u breaks collinearity only because the contract explicitly assumes X can vary while z stays fixed. For the stated z and u, the centered X′ has squared norm 12.8, z has squared norm 10, and their inner product is 10. The two-column Gram determinant is therefore 28, so the centered design has rank 2. The two model predictions differ by 2u. This algebra checks the proposed discriminator; it neither supplies an actual intervention nor validates a planetary or human manipulation.
