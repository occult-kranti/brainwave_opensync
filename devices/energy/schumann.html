02 / IDEAL CAVITY

There is more than one wavelength definition.

Ideal fℓ = c√[ℓ(ℓ+1)]/(2πR); horizontal scale λℓ = 2πR/√[ℓ(ℓ+1)]. The vacuum reference c/f is a different quantity. Real conductivity and dispersion shift the observed peaks; this lossless model is not an Earth fit.

04 / REVERSE TEST

One output can have two explanations.

If forcing is unknown, Sᶠ₂ = Sᶠ₁ |H₁/H₂|² makes two different responses produce the same output power spectrum on a band where the responses are nonzero. More output samples cannot remove that exact ambiguity.

With known mass and calibrated complex input–output response, the two synthetic natural frequencies are recovered and pass held-out numerical checks. Incorrect gain and multimode controls fail. An unconstrained frequency-dependent receiver still passes as a different oscillator, including at the same known mass. Stability and causality alone do not remove this ambiguity. No Earth parameters were inferred.

A controlled input–output experiment can identify parameters only under its calibration assumptions. The bench model is not a proposal to drive the Earth–ionosphere cavity, and it does not show that natural lightning follows the constructed forcing.

05 / KEEP THE ENERGY BOUNDARY

A field amplitude is still not a power budget.

In the stated vacuum approximation, uᴮ = B²/(2µ₀). For a zero-mean perturbation, the time-averaged cross term with a constant background vanishes. These are local magnetic energy densities, not global Earth energy or extractable watts.

A stationary coil in a static field has no changing flux. For a driven dissipative mode, load output requires input power or depletion of stored energy. A traveling-plane-wave flux formula is not justified for a general static or standing field.

06 / NEXT MEASUREMENT

What would make an anomaly test meaningful?

  1. Define the claim first: local fitted peak, common cavity pole, amplitude change or global event. Preselect station, channel, band, UTC interval, baseline and statistical decision rule.
  2. Obtain raw or calibrated spectral records with sensor orientation, gain and phase response, clock accuracy, sampling, windowing, missing-data and clipping flags. Preserve immutable source bytes and permission to reuse them.
  3. Use a matched seasonal/local-time baseline, a documented noise/source model, uncertainty propagation, dependence-aware statistics and correction for selected windows or multiple modes.
  4. Compare independent calibrated stations for a global claim. Model lightning geography, receiver response and interference before attributing a shift to a changed cavity.
  5. Validate the inverse method on a controlled low-energy mechanical or electrical analogue with a measured input and at least two distinct held-out frequencies. Distinguish bench validation from observational Earth identification.
  6. For any energy-collection proposal, measure source work, storage changes, dissipation and load power over a complete cycle; report uncertainty and a no-load control. Static magnetic density does not supply a power estimate.
