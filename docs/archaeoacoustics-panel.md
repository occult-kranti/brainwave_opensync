# Sound in Stone review

The starting material was a user-supplied digest without a video URL or transcript. Six AI reviewers covered South Asia and ritual practice, archaeological acoustics, wave physics and EEG, mathematical models, audio, and visual design. The moderator integrated the work. The reviews constitute source and software checks.

| Round | Artifact reviewed | Decision and verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Supplied claims and existing Open Sync routes | Verified primary or authoritative sources where available. Created six research chapters, the listening collection, mathematical benches and an editable exhibit flow. Site measurements carry methods or sample scope. Claims without traceable evidence carry explicit limits. |
| 2 | Integrated page, model and audio draft | Kept 394 Hz, scene and rendering constant in the comparison of two declared virtual plates. Fixed the stair time scale at 0–100 ms, added an ideal King’s Chamber box and stated its dimensions, and put captions and attribution beside artwork. Tied Schumann and solar references to their actual audio mappings. |
| 3 | Revised exhibit and all six chapter routes | Reviewed formula bounds, source links, image rights, preview lifecycle, chapter navigation and final content. Reproduction checks include endpoint sweeps on all model controls, an exact 394 Hz round/square recipe comparison, and research preview lifecycle tests. |

The [editable FigJam flow](https://www.figma.com/board/cjkKVwK0Pjcotnh0CaLHJb) maps research evidence, listening comparisons and cymatics. The [image credits](../public/archaeoacoustics/CREDITS.md) identify site imagery and the generated illustration. The previews synthesize short references. The room and staircase benches calculate idealized fields and path delays using stated assumptions.
