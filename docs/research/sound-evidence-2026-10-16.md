# Sound research source review — 16 October 2026

This is a targeted, source-led evidence update for the OpenSync sound research lab. It is not an exhaustive systematic review of all sound, music therapy, ultrasound, historical traditions or commercial products. The implementation source of truth is `src/sound-research/evidence.ts`.

## What was checked

Searches covered 2024–2026 randomized listening research, auditory beat experiments, EEG endpoints, sleep, patient-selected music, historical tuning claims, singing bowls, official medical acoustics records and hearing risk. Older studies were included when they supplied a useful comparator or direct test of a popular claim. Search engines were used for discovery; source cards rely on original papers, their PubMed abstracts, or official FDA/NIH material. Search cut-off: 2026-10-16.

Full-text availability is not the same as full-text verification. Each card records what was actually accessible. Several PubMed and publisher pages intermittently returned bot checks or errors; indexed abstracts were used where explicitly stated. No paywall was bypassed and no proprietary recording was obtained or copied.

## Claim boundaries

- **A**: established acoustics or well-supported hearing-risk facts. The FDA card grades the factual distinction between a regulated device and a listening app, not generic sound-treatment efficacy.
- **B**: some direct human evidence, including small pilots. It is not a claim of replication, high certainty or clinical validation of OpenSync.
- **C**: weak or indirect evidence for the stated interpretation; the five-person clinical extension is exploratory.
- **D**: folklore, numerology or an unsupported physiological interpretation. Cultural and commercial source cards are maintained separately.

All four new hypotheses are **Untested**. No study tested the new OpenSync presets. No audio setting is labeled as a disease treatment. The simulation represents signals and simple acoustic relationships; it does not predict a person's EEG, organs, infection, tumor response or recovery.

## Interpretation checks that affect the product

The 2025 monaural trial and 2026 music-plus-beats trial cannot isolate a beat effect without a music-only comparator. Our inference is therefore to include a matched sound-bed comparison in the hypothesis design, not to promote a new beat treatment. The older four-arm study offers a useful design precedent, with uncertainty from open-label administration and subgroup findings.

The 2026 publication describes recruitment in 2023. Its publication date is current; its data are not a newly collected 2026 cohort. The 2025 personalized-sound paper was electronically published in August and assigned to a December issue. Publication year must not be used as a quality ranking.

The 2019 tuning paper has a corrigendum that remains unread. Avoid promoting its exact effect estimate. A reference tuning changes all notes in a composition. Comparing two isolated tones does not replicate that study. A significance result within one condition and no significance in another would not by itself establish a significant between-condition difference.

The recent EEG study measured an electrophysiological response, not disease recovery or a validated wellness outcome. The open-label audiovisual extension used dedicated clinical equipment. It offers no basis for claiming that an ordinary audio-only preset recreates the intervention.

## Mathematical and biological separation

The waveform is an electrical/digital representation that a playback device converts into sound pressure. The ear transduces that mechanical input into neural signals. Attention, expectation, musical familiarity and context can also affect a listening report. Their contributions are not calculated by a waveform generator.

For two sinusoidal tones delivered together, the acoustic beat rate is the absolute frequency difference. In binaural presentation each ear receives a different tone; a plotted sum is a mathematical comparison, not a physical envelope delivered independently to each ear. Pitch, amplitude-modulation rate, musical tempo and measured EEG frequency are separate quantities even when their units can be converted to cycles per second.

Wavelength is speed divided by frequency for the chosen medium. It is not a tissue resonance target. Digital RMS and sample peak characterize the signal; without calibrated playback they do not determine pressure at the listener's ear. Equal digital RMS also does not guarantee equal perceived loudness.

## Low-risk hypothesis methodology

The four proposals are comparisons of everyday adult listening experience. Use comfortable levels, short sessions, optional participation and immediate stop on discomfort. Do not provoke pain, sleep deprivation or illness to obtain an outcome. Do not change medication or care. No participant recruitment or clinical trial is implemented here.

Before comparing conditions, select one primary rating and a fixed number of comparisons. Keep device, duration and setting consistent; randomize/counterbalance order; use neutral labels; capture starting mood and interruptions. Give a no-difference option, retain early stops, and include negative results. Audio identity can sometimes be guessed, so do not call a condition double-blind without verifying masking.

Compute paired differences in pre/post ratings and show their spread, session count and missing observations. A private observation log is exploratory and cannot establish clinical efficacy or a population effect. Do not cherry-pick the best session, turn correlation into mechanism, or fabricate EEG/biological data. Comfortable listening and preference are legitimate outcomes in their own right.
