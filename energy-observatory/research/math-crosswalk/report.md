**Thermal channel.** For excited population p, damping γ and stationary excited population ν, p′=(1−γ)p+γν and Q_into=hfγ(ν−p). Coherence is multiplied by √(1−γ), followed by the declared dephasing factor. Full-rank bath cases satisfy the tested entropy-production inequality. ν>1/2 is an inverted resource. Two independent reviewers checked six and three separately frozen states; the latter gave 63 numerical comparisons. Preparation, switching, bath reset, cooling and measurement costs remain outside this ideal channel. Ergotropy is not the communication-capacity theorem.

**Acoustics.** At fixed geometry and Poisson ratio, scaling E and ρ by the same positive factor leaves elastic wave speeds and modeled modal frequencies unchanged. With E=123.5 GPa, ρ=8960 kg/m³, R=0.04445 m and ν=0.34, the first modeled radial frequency is about 45,495.335 Hz. Doubling E and ρ preserves it; imposed isotropic strain 10⁻⁶I doubles static local traction from 385,937.5 Pa to 771,875 Pa. Net hydrostatic force over the closed sphere is zero: the discriminator is calibrated local traction or force on a known patch. These are hypothetical parameter candidates, not a claim that moving copper changes its density. Known density already removes this specific ambiguity; neither condition proves general inverse uniqueness. Unknown sensor gain can hide amplitude information.

**Exact energy identity.** For H=kq²/2+p²/(2m), q̇=p/m, ṗ=−kq−bp/m+u and output y=p/m, Ḣ=uy−by². Exact coefficient cancellation and rational matrices agree for m,k>0 and b≥0. Wrong port, negative damping, perturbed Gram factors and a polynomial positive on sampled endpoints but negative between them were rejected. This is an established identity newly checked in our workflow, not a novel theorem or Lean execution.

**Independent skepticism.** Hₙ=diag(0,1/n,1) has a constant ground-state projector but no positive uniform gap. Distinct matrices diag(1,2,3) and diag(1,2,4) have identical resolvents after projection onto the first two coordinates, at every positive spectral parameter. The explicit formulas establish these narrow countermodels; finite samples alone do not prove their all-parameter conclusions. They expose missing implications, not counterexamples to external theorems with stronger data.
