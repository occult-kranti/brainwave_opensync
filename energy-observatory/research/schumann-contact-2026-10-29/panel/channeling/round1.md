2. Third-party transcripts surfaced a specific 7.85 Hz attribution and putative alien-world frequencies. Recording identity, edition, transcription fidelity and original context remain unchecked. 3. **Exact recent prior art:** Wahbeh–Glick (2026) prospectively collected standardized interviews from three purposively selected channelers on 28 topics, then compared sentence embeddings. Reported correspondence is 0.65–0.79 across channelers for the same purported ETI and 0.75–0.88 within channelers across ETIs after residualization. The companion qualitative study treats the material as discourse and reports no explicitly falsifiable test supplied in its verification question. These findings are not independent contact observations. Direct publisher HTML returned 403; the abstract and selected publisher-indexed Methods/Discussion passages were inspected, not the full data/code or all controls. [CH04–CH05]
4. **Existing channeling controls are informative but mixed.** A 2019 study of 13 trance channels found voice differences but no substantive differences in its other measured physiological channels. A 2023 matched-question study of 15 channelers reported little cross-channeler answer correspondence. New 2026 EEG-network work (14 mediums, 14 controls) addresses task-associated brain patterns, not externally verified information. A 2025 single-case EEG paper's rejection of “fraud” does not follow from EEG alone; the inspected abstract itself leaves ESP unresolved. [CH06–CH09]
5. Positive blinded mediumship results exist: Tressoldi and colleagues report 65% correct intended-reading identification across 100 readings from 28 mediums. Publication appeared online in 2022 and in a 2025 issue. This is an adjacent deceased-person-information task, not ET source identification or a Schumann experiment; we inspected the primary abstract, not its complete trial audit. [CH10]
6. AIR's 1995 remote-viewing evaluation preserves a distinction frequently lost in later retellings: statistical laboratory departures and their interpretation remained disputed, while the evaluated operational material lacked the specificity/reliability needed for intelligence use. Selected executive-summary pages of the primary report were read via the National Security Archive copy. Neither this report nor government custody identifies extraterrestrial agency. [CH11]
7. Wang et al. (2019) used controlled rotations of roughly 35 µT fields over 100 ms and EEG/sham/phantom controls. That experiment is not exposure to a picotesla Schumann oscillation. Alpha-band readout does not make its magnetic stimulus an 8–13 Hz Earth-cavity carrier. Selected primary methods and results were read; no replication is claimed. [CH12]

## Next discriminating contract

Before human data collection, freeze an independently generated, hidden target among K equiprobable candidates per trial. Keep target custodian, participant/session facilitator, scorer and analyst separated until immutable responses and analysis are locked. Primary endpoint: preselected target rank or exact forced-choice correctness; do not score subjective narrative resonance as success. Include matched non-channeling responses, sham target assignments, all misses/abstentions, complete trial IDs and the planned stopping rule. Group repeated trials by participant/session; predefine multiplicity handling, exclusions and a holdout replication. Target secrecy, timestamps and blinded scoring need audit evidence, not merely metadata assertions.

An observational Schumann association would require an additional predeclared channel/component, site, timestamp accuracy, calibration, baseline, nuisance controls, lag and comparison family. The protocol should first test whether ordinary leakage, selection, autocorrelation or repeated scans produce the apparent effect. A positive result would support only the specified information/association effect pending replication; it would not by itself distinguish ET, other proposed agency, or an overlooked ordinary mechanism.
