## Ordinary counterexamples to the proposed cross-link

**Shared time structure.** Let a calendar/environment nuisance be z(t), an observed field statistic X(t)=a z(t)+ε(t), and report rate Y(t)=b z(t)+η(t). With mutually independent zero-mean noises independent of z, Cov(X,Y)=ab Var(z). A correlation therefore occurs without a causal X→Y edge. This is an elementary declared model, not a fit to the user's data. Clock time, seasonal lightning, sleep/wake schedules, reporting schedules and internet exposure are distinct candidate nuisances requiring measurement, not assumed explanations of a particular person.

**Selection.** Choosing the brightest image after seeing a contact report, then trying frequency bands, time lags, planetary calendars and scoring rules, selects on a joint outcome. The analysis family includes all those choices. A corrected test must rerun the entire chosen search under the null or use an untouched confirmatory target. The often-quoted expression 1−(1−α)^m applies to independent tests only; real correlated windows do not justify substituting their count into that formula. Bonferroni's family bound is conservative but does not require test independence, provided each individual p-value is valid.

**Unidentified agency.** An unexpected score, a genuine field association, or even a repeatable physical coupling would not identify an extraterrestrial agent. Competing ordinary information pathways and the physical source would remain separate hypotheses. This inference distinction follows the intervention/association separation in [SK-YUAN-SHOU-2022]; it is not a statement about the meaning someone assigns to a spiritual experience.

**Spectrogram ambiguity.** Display brightness, missing data, receiver gain or broadband pickup can change independently of an intrinsic cavity pole. Kp and Schumann frequency are different observables. The previous study already preserved those limitations; a new graph must not erase them.

## Recommended Round 2 contract

Question: **Can the proposed cross-domain analysis reject a deliberately shared-calendar synthetic null without mistaking it for an ET signal?** This is a software-control benchmark, not new physics.

Before execution, freeze the time grid, random seed, latent common driver, noise model, missingness rule, candidate lag/band family, statistic and pass/fail criteria. Produce at least: independent-noise null, shared-driver null, direct-coupling positive control, and an artifact/pickup case. Keep truth labels hidden from the scoring function. A positive control validates detection in its stated model, not biological plausibility.

Compare naive correlation to one predeclared nuisance-adjusted statistic. If using resampling, state and test the exact invariance assumption; do not call pointwise shuffles valid for autocorrelated records. For a phase/circular null, use an explicitly periodic stationary synthetic model and preserve the appropriate amplitudes/covariance. Show a nonstationary example where that assumption is broken, or mark it outside scope. Report finite Monte Carlo precision and include the observed configuration; never report a zero p-value from a finite null sample.

Keep astrology-sim-ant's astronomical coordinates/calendar features as candidate covariates with pinned timezone/ephemeris settings, not verified causal factors. No missing ephemeris or measurement may be filled with a symbolic correspondence.

Admission criteria: numerical correctness; unchanged null/held-out contracts; rejection of invalid input claims; and clear presentation that real field–contact association and ET attribution remain untested. The next round must be chosen after inspecting the actual result and objections.
