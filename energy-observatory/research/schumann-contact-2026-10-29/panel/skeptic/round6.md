## Commitment disclosure test

The target vocabulary is four literal UTF-8 strings: copper, silver, gold and iron. Hashing each and comparing with the disclosed digest recovers gold. This is a four-candidate dictionary search, not a hash-function break. It demonstrates why an unsalted digest of a tiny public target space fails to conceal a target.

The calculation does not prove that a proposed salted implementation is secure. A nonce must be unpredictable, sufficiently entropic and secret until the allowed reveal; domain separation, target encoding, custody and original chronology need independent review. No live target, secret or human-session record was generated in this demonstration.

## Protocol clarification retained

My review identified a distinction between the Round5 exact forced-choice model and Round6 abstention accounting. If every trial contains an actual K-way guess under the conditional-uniform target null, the hit count has the declared binomial law. If pre-reveal abstentions are retained as nonhits, their hit probability is0; the same binomial upper tail is conservative under those unpredictability assumptions, not generally the exact hit-count distribution. Root incorporated this clarification into `protocol.md` and the round contract. The validator itself calculates no p-value.
