# Source and prior-art gate before another research cycle

Reviewer: `/root/oct_sources`. Assessment date: 2026-10-29. This is a literature and applicability review, not a fourth research round. It is separate from final acceptance of the R3 implementation.

**No-go for another cycle presented as discovery of the same oscillator, spectral-compensation, coherence or calibrated-inverse results.** The completed calculations are useful verified teaching and diagnostic examples, but their mathematical content is contained in established response and identification theory. No new Schumann measurement or physical theorem has been established. A next cycle requires a precisely specified empirical, metrological or comparative-method deliverable, a closest-prior-art comparison, and advisor admission before execution.

## Restricted identification already succeeds in prior work

The arbitrary-input counterexample must not be marketed as disproving operational modal analysis. Brincker et al.'s frequency-domain decomposition derives its exact decomposition under white excitation, light damping and geometric orthogonality of closely spaced mode shapes; otherwise it is approximate. Its pole/residue decomposition and spectral matrix are materially richer than a single unknown scalar PSD. The body and equations, not only the abstract, were inspected.

Basseville et al. formulate Gaussian white process excitation and covariance/subspace identification. Their Eqs. 6–9 factor a covariance Hankel matrix into observability and controllability terms; identifying the state transition requires the relevant observability rank. Section 3 also treats measured input together with unmeasured noise under explicit whiteness and independence assumptions. These conditions are not supplied by our arbitrary source PSD. Their success therefore does not contradict our example, and our example does not negate their method.

Likewise, H=Lxy=Gxy/Gxx represents an actual physical transfer only under the measurement assumptions. Howes et al. state linear time invariance, absence of omitted correlated inputs, negligible input noise and output noise uncorrelated with the input. Our arithmetic agreement does not certify those conditions in a copper-ball instrument, an electromagnetic receiver or an Earth station.
