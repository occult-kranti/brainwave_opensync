# Schumann panel — source quality and recent-event provenance

Reviewer: oct_sources. Date: 2026-10-29. Independent first panel report. The question is whether a recent change in a calibrated Earth–ionosphere resonance observable is demonstrated during October 23–29, with October 27–29 examined separately.

## What the sources permit

The primary **Space Observing System 70** site publishes a rolling sonogram and separate frequency, amplitude and Q panels for three experimental days. Its page says week-long graphical/numeric products are offered through a subscription. This reviewer did not access that product, did not infer inaccessible values, and did not republish its copyrighted images. Its explanation identifies black vertical bands as missing recording and distinguishes resonance bands, local lightning and technological interference. A bright column is not by itself a globally changed eigenfrequency.

The primary site's measurement-method page says actual acquisition uses 80 Hz sampling, spectra use 50-second windows advanced by 5 seconds, and nominal frequency-bin spacing is 0.02 Hz. Reported mode frequencies are estimated spectral maxima, and mode amplitude is the square root of the spectral-density maximum. These are source-stated processing details; they are not a calibration certificate or an uncertainty bound for every published frame. The ADC's separate 150 kHz specification must not be substituted for the acquisition rate.

Primary URLs inspected: https://sos70.ru/ ; https://sos70.ru/?page_id=43 ; https://sos70.ru/?page_id=48 . Reading depth: page text and method description; no raw time series analyzed and no image pixels interpreted as calibrated field strength. The older sosrff.tsu.ru page timed out in this attempt; that timeout establishes neither a station outage nor a physical anomaly.

The **Nagycenk observatory** provides a public Schumann page with chart links. The retrieved page text did not expose a dated numeric time series or a complete seven-day archive. Its institutional provenance is useful, but this review did not establish the current chart's dates or infer numerical anomalies from it. Source: https://nckobs.hu/data/sr/ .

## Official solar/geomagnetic context is a different observable

NOAA SWPC's forecast discussion issued **2026-10-29 00:30 UTC** reports quiet geomagnetic conditions over its preceding summary interval, a C1.0 flare at 2026-10-28 06:13 UTC, and no Earth-directed CME in the available imagery. It forecasts possible minor geomagnetic storms on **October 31**, outside the requested retrospective window. The forecast must not be reported as a storm already observed on October 27–29.

Source: https://services.swpc.noaa.gov/text/discussion.txt . Its solar-wind/IMF nT values are not the local terrestrial background magnetic field and are not Schumann-mode magnetic amplitudes. Even a verified Kp event would be context rather than proof of a cavity-frequency shift. This review did not inspect every earlier daily NOAA bulletin.

## Secondary claims discovered, not admitted measurements

Search results surfaced October 27 claims of “very high” amplitude, relative multipliers and different daily/current peak frequencies on commercial aggregators. Some disclose pixel extraction or custom scores; others offer wellbeing interpretations. These are leads requiring a source-to-value reconstruction, a baseline definition, timestamp conversion and uncertainty. No displayed activity category is a universal Schumann scale.

Examples were inspected at search-result level only: mind.university/today/schumann-resonance/2026-10-27, sungeo.net/archive, schumannresonance.app, ressonanciaschumannhoje.com/en. They are not used as physical evidence. Apparent disagreements may reflect different times, daily averages, sensors, extraction methods or scores; they do not establish global disagreement in Earth's frequency.

## Scientific grounding and admission

NASA NTRS document 20120000051 describes lightning-excited Earth–ionosphere electromagnetic modes and satellite signatures. Its abstract is relevant physical grounding, not evidence about October 2026. This review read the NASA record and abstract, not a new observational dataset: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20120000051 .

**Decision: no recent Schumann anomaly is admitted by this source review.** This means insufficient independently checked primary numerical evidence, not proof that no anomaly happened. Accept the primary-source distinctions and the dated NOAA contextual statement. The observation specialist may provide additional timestamped evidence under the same contract.

Next discriminating inputs: station-native numerical E/B spectra or time series, exact observation timestamps, calibration/transfer functions, missing/saturated interval flags, comparison baseline, and an independent station. Analyze peak position, amplitude and linewidth separately; preserve local interference and lightning explanations. No health, acoustic, source-free-energy or Yang–Mills inference follows from overlapping frequency numbers.
