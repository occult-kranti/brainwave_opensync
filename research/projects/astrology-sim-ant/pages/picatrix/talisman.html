The Talisman Workflow

Choose an aim and a moment; this guided flow computes the election, gathers the historical correspondences, finds the Moon's mansion and the decan image, and assembles what the sources record about such a working into an end-result card — every line third-person, attributed and cited, all computed on the verified engine. The site reports what the texts directed their operator to do; it does not direct you to do anything.

Honest note This is presented strictly as a historical and cultural artefact — the documented practice of medieval and Renaissance magicians — described, not prescribed. Talismanic magic has no demonstrated efficacy; some historical recipes name materials that are toxic or illegal and are recorded for study only, never as instruction. See Sources & Science.

A worked example — a Venus talisman for concord

The classic illustration: a talisman of Venus for love & concord (Picatrix III, Venus).

  1. Aim & planet: Picatrix III assigns concord to Venus.
  2. The election: the tradition elects the day and hour of Venus, with the Moon waxing and swift, neither void-of-course nor in the via combusta (Spica exempt), Venus dignified and free of Mars and Saturn, and a benefic on an angle. The engine scans the next 72 hours and returns the best window the rules allow.
  3. The Moon: those texts prefer a mansion of love and concord (e.g. Mansion 13, Al Awwa), a decan face of Venus, and Spica or Sirius conjunct Venus or the Moon where one is available.
  4. Materials (historical): the materials recorded are a suffumigation of aloes, mastic and roses, the colour white, the metal copper and the stone emerald. The archaic measures are not reliably convertible and are not converted here.
  5. Powers (kept distinct): the Picatrix prayer-angel is Beyteyl; Agrippa's separate triad is Angel Haniel, Intelligence Hagiel, Spirit Kedemel.
  6. Design: Agrippa (Three Books II.22–46) gives the image of Venus engraved in copper at the elected hour.
