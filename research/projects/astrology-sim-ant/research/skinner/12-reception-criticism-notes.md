## How the research actually went

### 1. The continuity thesis — what it is

Anchored, unusually well, in a public university record: the PhD thesis at Newcastle (NSW), repository
id **uon:13943**, title *Magical techniques and implements present in Graeco-Egyptian magical papyri,
Byzantine Greek Solomonic manuscripts and European grimoires: transmission, continuity and commonality
(the technology of Solomonic magic)*, degree 2014, published in expanded halves as *Techniques of
Graeco-Egyptian Magic* (2014) and *Techniques of Solomonic Magic* (2015), Golden Hoard / Llewellyn.
The claim's own shape (per the thesis title, the Lycourinos digest, and the reviews): **technique- and
implement-level** continuity, PGM → Hygromanteia → Clavicula, with the operational rationale constant
and the theology/nomina/materia swapped out. The Golden Hoard Hygromanteia translation (Marathakis 2011)
wears the claim as its subtitle — *The True Ancestor of the Key of Solomon* — which matters for
provenance: the key intermediary text's English framing is published by Skinner's own imprint.

### 2. The academic response — thinner than the brief hoped

The brief listed the field's journals and names. The honest result:

- **No journal review located** of either *Techniques* volume in Aries, MRW, Correspondences, Ambix,
  Preternature, La Rosa di Paracelso. Multiple query formulations, including site-restricted. Skinner's
  own reviews page corroborates the gap negatively — it aggregates Amazon and trade notices only.
- **None of the named scholars** (Kieckhefer, Klaassen, Page, Davies, Fanger, Otto, Faraone, Torallas
  Tovar, Peterson) found engaging the thesis in openly accessible print. Otto's *Aries* 16 (2016)
  historicising article is the likeliest venue and is paywalled — flagged unresolved, not asserted
  either way.
- The substantive published engagements found are: **Harms** (two blog reviews, 2014/2015 — the single
  most useful critical source: thesis "not a new hypothesis"; no collation of actual prayers/invocations,
  i.e. the typology-vs-philology objection), **Lycourinos** in the Brill *Dictionary of Contemporary
  Esotericism* (the "scholar-magician" classification; Skinner "seeks to restore the core of Western
  magic"), the **Asprem** caution quoted therein ("evidence is scant at times" for unbroken-tradition
  claims), and **Karr's** bibliographic survey (favourable; one merkavah/Zoharariel quibble).
- One formal validation exists: the thesis **passed examination in Classics**. That is a fact about
  process, not a peer-review of the published argument, and the JSON says so.

**For the operative graph:** any Skinner-sourced transmission edge is a *claimedBy* edge with contested
status. The two positions to carry: Skinner 2014/2015 (systematic technique-level continuity) vs Harms
2015 (lineage not new; textual collation absent). Never resolved, per FRAMING §1.3/§2.4.

### 3. Practitioner vs philologist

The cleanest documented statement of the split is Lycourinos's taxonomy itself (scholars vs
scholar-magicians). On the practitioner side the Spiral Nature review of *Techniques of Solomonic Magic*
is gold for this repo's purposes: it praises the book precisely because corpus-wide comparison lets
practitioners **"fill in missing details and combine techniques from multiple grimoires into a coherent
whole."** That is completion-by-inference plus aggregation — the two moves FRAMING §5 (A-1/A-2) exists
to forbid. Skinner's reception is a live demonstration of why a comparative apparatus is operable
capability, independent of its author's intent. Quote the *reception*, never model the move.

Academic-side pattern across Harms 2014/2015/2020 + Karr: praise for manuscript access, surveys of
text-families, apparatus, usefulness; criticism concentrated on witness selection (Ars Notoria built on
Agrippa's *Opera omnia* + Turner 1657 instead of the medieval MSS), single-reading interpretive choices
("Euclid of Thebes"), speculative identifications (Constantinople cistern Medusae), and transcription
method. Peterson's Lemegeton remains Karr's benchmark edition; Skinner–Rankine's *Goetia of Dr Rudd*
is valued for unique manuscript content (Harley 6483 + Harley 6482, Sloane 3824, Wellcome 32).

### 4. Geomancy and feng shui — the academically-received corner, now with real citations

- **Winder, *Medical History* 26:1 (1982) 107–108** (PMC1139129 — full scan read this session): chief
  merit is the first cross-cultural comparison of eastern and western geomancies; criticism is the
  unused/unlisted Wellcome MSS 10, 394, 531; verdict "a very informative book on a little-known
  subject." Bonus for the atlas: the review records the book's own diffusion argument — Arabian origin,
  three routes (trans-Saharan; Red Sea/Indian Ocean to Madagascar; Muslim Spain into Europe) — which is
  exactly transmission-edge-shaped material, citable as *Skinner's claim as reported by Winder*.
- **Theobald, *EASTM* 37 (2013), from p. 111**, on *Guide to the Feng Shui Compass*: "probably the most
  important Western scholar taking the science of Fengshui serious[ly]" — with abstract-level criticisms
  (over-technical latter half, sourcing of Chinese texts, geographic inaccuracies, illustration choices).
  Full text 403'd at Brill; abstract-derived points flagged.
- So the brief's premise held, with nuance: the geomancy/feng-shui wing is where Skinner's work meets
  peer-reviewed journals *at all* — but as **reviews of reference value with named defects**, not as
  uncontested authority.
